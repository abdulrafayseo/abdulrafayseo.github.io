Context

An established US reseller of refurbished enterprise IT hardware — servers, storage and networking parts, thousands of SKUs, long sales cycles and a buyer who often searches by part number rather than by category. Paid search and organic were being measured separately, so nobody could say which one was actually producing the pipeline.

The problem

Organic demand for the catalogue was shrinking: impressions over the measured six months came in at 5.22M against 6.57M in the six months before, and clicks fell from 61.7K to 51.9K. Treating that as a ranking failure would have been the wrong read — the pages were holding their positions, the market around them had contracted. The question was whether the account could convert a smaller audience better, and whether paid could carry the volume the category no longer gave away.

Approach

Two tracks, measured against each other. On organic: fix what controls click-through rather than chase lost impressions — product schema and rich snippets on the catalogue, crawl budget pointed at pages that convert, HTTPS and Core Web Vitals cleaned up so nothing was being held back technically. On paid: restructure campaigns around the part-number searches that actually convert, and report everything against a single cost-per-conversion figure so spend decisions stopped being argued from impressions.

My role

I ran both tracks end to end for this account: the technical SEO audit and its fixes, the schema and snippet work, the Google Ads campaign structure, and the reporting the two were judged by.
