Context

A London company that manufactures and installs aluminium and uPVC windows and doors. Established business, real premises, trade accreditations on the wall, a site that had been rebuilt not long before and looked the part. They came for a site audit because the website was producing nothing and nobody could say why.

The problem

The site had been launched but never finished. It was built on the temporary address the hosting provider supplies before a domain is pointed at it, and that move was only half done: 217 links across the site still pointed at the old address, including every item in the Products menu and the Browse Products button on the homepage. Thirteen brochure downloads failed for the same reason. The contact email printed on every page was still on that temporary subdomain, so it looked like spam to anyone who read it and went nowhere if they used it. Separately, the Get a Free Quote button - the primary enquiry route on a site where every page ends in a call to action - fired a popup that had been deleted, so the page just reloaded. None of this was visible from looking at the site.

Approach

A full technical, on-page, off-page and UX review of all 17 pages, then a prioritised plan rather than a list. The order mattered more than the length: fix what breaks a customer journey first, what Google reads second, what loads third. The staging links and the dead quote button came first because both cost enquiries on the day. The missing H1s came next, and they were cheap - the titles and meta descriptions were already well written and London-targeted, so the heading level was the only part missing. Page weight came third, led by a 676 KB animated GIF being displayed at 102 by 102 pixels and more than thirty render-blocking stylesheets.

My role

The audit and the plan were mine: the crawl, the findings, the priority order and the report. This engagement was a diagnosis, not a retainer, so the figures below are what the audit found, not results after a fix. The report also made clear what was already right, because a list that says everything is broken is not a diagnosis: all 17 pages returned 200 with correct canonicals, titles and meta descriptions were unique and well targeted on every page, the service pages ran 1,000 to 1,240 words, and the backlink profile was healthy with no toxicity found.

Results
