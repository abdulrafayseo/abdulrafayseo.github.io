Context

A single-practitioner counselling practice with a WordPress site. Seven indexable pages, warm and genuinely well-written copy, real testimonials, transparent pricing, and an accredited practitioner behind it. Everything a person looking for a counsellor would want to read was already on the page. Almost nobody was reaching it.

The problem

Google had nothing to hold on to. Not one of the seven pages carried an H1, and not one carried a meta description - the only page on the whole domain with an H1 was WordPress's own default Sample Page, still sitting there unpublished from install day. Six inner pages reused the homepage hero, so the first heading Google read on Services, About, FAQ, Blog, Testimonials and Contact was the same greeting every time. Page titles were one-word labels. Every counselling format and modality the practice offered shared a single URL, so none of them could rank for its own term, and the eighteen blog items were text blocks stacked on one page rather than articles. The index recorded no organic keywords and no organic traffic at all.

Approach

A full technical, on-page, content and authority review of all seven pages, then a prioritised plan. Most of this was cheap to fix, which is worth saying plainly: an H1 and a meta description per page, titles that name the service instead of labelling it, one URL per counselling format, and the blog split into real articles. The 11.8 MB of PNG images were next. The authority problem was the slow one - 105 referring domains at an authority score of 2 is a profile that needs replacing rather than extending, and that takes months, not an afternoon.

My role

The audit and the plan were mine: the crawl, the findings, the priority order and the report. This was a diagnosis, not a retainer, so the figures below are what the audit found rather than results after a fix. The report said what was already right too: HTTPS throughout, all seven pages returning 200, indexable and correctly canonicalised, a clean robots.txt, layout stability close to perfect at 0.001 to 0.002 cumulative layout shift with zero blocking time, and Lighthouse scores of 96 for best practices and 92 for SEO. The writing was the strongest asset on the site; the markup was hiding it.

Results

Account names, property URLs, campaign names and brand queries are blurred at the client's expense of recognition, not at the numbers' expense: every figure below is unretouched.
