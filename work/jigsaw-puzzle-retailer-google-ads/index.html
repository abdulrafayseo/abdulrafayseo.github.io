Context

An online retailer selling jigsaw puzzles, running three campaigns: Performance Max and Standard Shopping across the catalogue, and a Search campaign on non-brand general terms. A share of the search demand arrives in Chinese rather than English, which matters more than it sounds - a feed and a keyword set built only for English terms simply does not see that traffic.

The problem

The account was returning well on a small budget and was capped by it. Two of the three campaigns sat in Limited by budget or Limited by search volume, which are different problems wearing the same label: the first means Google would spend more if allowed, the second means there is not enough matching demand at the current targeting. Treating both as a budget problem wastes money on the second. The account also had the usual Performance Max difficulty - it reports as one number, so without separating it from Shopping and Search you cannot tell which part of a rising spend is paying.

Approach

Budget released first on the campaign that was genuinely budget-limited, and the targeting widened on the one that was volume-limited rather than throwing money at it. Performance Max, Shopping and Search kept separate so each one's return could be read on its own instead of being averaged into a single account figure. The product feed was treated as the lever it is on a catalogue account: titles and attributes that match how buyers actually search, in both languages the demand arrives in.
