Context

A parts distributor whose buyers search almost entirely by part number and by cross-reference - the number printed on the old part, or the equivalent code from another manufacturer. That makes the catalogue the whole site: thousands of pages, each one competing for a handful of very specific searches, and almost no category-level demand to fall back on.

The problem

A part-number catalogue has a particular failure mode. Each page is cheap to publish and almost identical to its neighbours, so a site accumulates thousands of near-duplicate pages that the crawler never finishes and the index never fully trusts. This one was ranking at an average position of 8.4 and converting 3.92M impressions into 52.2K clicks. The pages existed and were indexable; what they lacked was the structured data that makes a part listing usable in results, and the crawl priorities to tell Google which of several thousand near-identical pages mattered.

Approach

Product schema and breadcrumbs across the catalogue so each part listing could carry its identifiers and its place in the structure into the results page; merchant listings cleaned up; crawl stats used to find where budget was being spent on pages that never convert and redirect it to the ones that do; and Core Web Vitals and HTTPS cleared so nothing was holding the catalogue back technically. On a catalogue this size the win is cumulative rather than dramatic - a small improvement repeated across thousands of pages.

My role

This account was mine end to end: the technical audit, the schema work, the crawl priorities and the reporting. The figures below are the client's own Search Console numbers over the six months of the work against the six before it, read off the property and not rounded. One of them went the wrong way and is published anyway: click-through rate fell from 1.3 per cent to 1.1 per cent. Impressions rose 56 per cent, which on a catalogue means ranking for a much wider set of part numbers, including many where the searcher wanted a different supplier. A wider net catches more fish and a smaller share of what it passes. Clicks still rose by 15.7K, which is the number that matters, but the rate fell and saying otherwise would be dishonest.
