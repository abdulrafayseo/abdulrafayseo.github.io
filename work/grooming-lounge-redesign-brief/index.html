Context

A high-end grooming lounge with a central London address, a founder with a serious industry reputation, a treatment menu running from £25 brow tidies to £250 signature facials, and a 5.0 rating from 632 reviews on its booking platform. The website showed almost none of it. They had already decided to rebuild, and wanted the audit to say what the new site had to fix rather than repeat.

The problem

Seventy-five treatments shared one Services page. The £150 to £250 skin treatments had no page of their own, so nothing in the highest-margin part of the menu could rank for its own term. Ten service pages that did exist ran about a hundred words each with a copied meta description, and thirteen more existed with nothing linking to them. The same haircut was listed twice at two different prices, £80 in one section and £90 in another. WordPress's Sample Page, the cart and the checkout were all still being submitted to Google, eight of the fourteen journal posts were hidden, and the FAQ advertised treatments the menu did not offer. Meanwhile 632 five-star reviews sat on the booking platform and two testimonials sat on the site.

Approach

Because the rebuild was already agreed, this one is a brief rather than a patch list. The structure comes first: one page per treatment group, with the high-value treatments getting their own pages and their own copy, which is the single change that unlocks the rest. Then housekeeping - the duplicate price resolved, the orphaned and thin pages either written properly or removed, the Sample Page and the cart out of the sitemap, the hidden journal posts published. Then proof, which is the cheapest win on the list: the reputation already exists, 632 reviews of it, and none of it was on the website. Schema last, since there was none of any kind.

My role

The audit and the redesign brief were mine. This was a diagnosis, not a retainer, so the figures below are what the audit found rather than results after a rebuild. The report was clear about what already worked and should survive the redesign: homepage, Services and Contact all had proper titles, H1s and meta descriptions written for the local search terms, the sitemap was clean, address, hours and phone were in every footer, booking ran properly through the platform, and the interior photography genuinely sells the place. The About page tells the story well. It just was not reaching anyone.

Results

Account names, property URLs, campaign names and brand queries are blurred at the client's expense of recognition, not at the numbers' expense: every figure below is unretouched.
