Context

A build-to-rent residential development letting studio, one-bed and two-bed apartments directly to tenants. Of the sites audited that quarter this one was performing best: 643 monthly organic visits from 180 ranking keywords, homepage copy properly targeted at the terms tenants actually search, and 3D virtual tours for every apartment type that most competing developments did not have.

The problem

Everything wrong with it was something nobody tidied up after launch. The browser showed Not Secure, because 23 pages still pulled resources over plain HTTP - the site logo among them, hard-coded with an http address. A prospective tenant was being asked to enter their name, phone number and email on a page the browser had flagged as unsafe. Four of the five blog posts still sat on the theme's demo URLs, so a local area guide lived at /the-number-1-secret-of-success/, and two of them displayed 2018 publication dates. Every apartment type existed three times over at competing URLs, twelve where four would do. Four decorative icons were still being loaded from the theme developer's own demo site, and the contact map pointed at an address in the United States.

Approach

A full technical, on-page, off-page and UX review of 27 pages, then a prioritised plan. The mixed content came first and alone, because a Not Secure warning on the page carrying the enquiry form costs lettings that day and everything else is slower-acting by comparison. The duplicate apartment URLs came second, consolidating twelve into four so the pages stopped competing with each other. The demo slugs, the 2018 dates, the borrowed icons and the American map pin were third - each one small, all of them together the difference between a site that looks maintained and one that does not.

My role

The audit and the plan were mine: the crawl, the findings, the priority order and the report. This was a diagnosis, not a retainer, so the figures below are what the audit found rather than results after a fix. This site was also the clearest case for saying what already worked: it genuinely ranks, both traffic and keywords were still growing, the page titles were well targeted, cumulative layout shift was zero on both mobile and desktop, and the virtual tours, named testimonials and working contact form were all real assets. The job here was tidying, not rebuilding.
