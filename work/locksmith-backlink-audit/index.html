Context

A locksmith with a small, ordinary link profile that had sat under ten referring domains for eleven months. Then, over about three weeks in August, it went vertical. The owner had not bought links and did not know where they had come from. They wanted to know whether it mattered.

The problem

It mattered. The profile was rated high toxicity, the most serious rating the tool gives. Of the 220 referring domains analysed, 199 were new and 117 had arrived in a single week. 62 per cent were toxic or potentially toxic: a private blog network of at least 26 sites, casino and betting domains, a pharmacy spam site and a psilocybin seller, all pointing at a locksmith. Ninety per cent of referring domains scored in the lowest authority band. The top linking country was Moldova. A quarter of the backlinks used link-selling anchor text, and one anchor read, in full, "Buy Backlinks Online Cheap" - it appeared 38 times. Through all of it the authority score had not moved off 2, and three new backlinks appeared on the day of the audit itself, so it was still growing.

Approach

Two questions had to be answered in order, and the second one only matters once the first is settled: is this a bulk purchase someone made, or a negative SEO attack someone aimed? The shape answers it - flat for a year then a near-vertical spike is what both look like, so the audit does not guess. What it does instead is quantify the damage, separate the toxic domains from the merely low quality, map the anchor text, and set out a recovery plan in the order it has to happen: disavow the confirmed toxic domains first, monitor weekly while the profile is still growing, and only then start building the links that should have been there all along.

My role

The audit and the recovery plan were mine. This was a diagnosis, not a retainer, so the figures below are what the audit found rather than results after a clean-up. Worth stating plainly: the report distinguishes between the two figure sets the tool reports - backlink analytics showed 233 referring domains and 248 backlinks, while the audit verified 220 domains and 231 backlinks as live at the time of the check. Toxicity figures come from the audit set, shape figures from the analytics set, and the report says which is which rather than quoting whichever is larger.
