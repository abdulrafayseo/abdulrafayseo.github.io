Context

An online retailer selling boat parts, engine components and watersports gear into a market where buyers search by part number and by engine brand as often as by category. Thousands of SKUs, a resource library of manuals and guides alongside the catalogue, and traffic arriving from several countries. The catalogue was already indexed; it was not being found.

The problem

The store was collecting impressions and converting almost none of them into visits. Over the six months before the work, 2.57M impressions produced 29.1K clicks - a click-through rate of 1.1 per cent - at an average position of 15, which is the middle of page two. A listing on page two with a thin snippet is invisible twice over: too low to be seen, and too bare to be clicked when it is. The product markup was the specific weakness. Merchant listings carried validation errors, so the results that did appear showed without the price, rating and availability that make a product listing worth clicking.

Approach

The work went after click-through rate and position together, because on a catalogue this size they are the same problem. Product and review schema across the catalogue so listings could carry price, rating and stock state into the results page; merchant listing errors cleared so the items were eligible for rich results at all; breadcrumbs so the category structure was legible to Google rather than just to a visitor; then crawl budget pointed at the pages that convert, Core Web Vitals and HTTPS cleaned up so nothing was being held back technically. The resource library - manuals and guides the catalogue pages could not rank for - was treated as its own entry point rather than as filler.

My role

This account was mine end to end: the technical audit, the schema and merchant listing work, the catalogue structure and the reporting.
